Structural drying is the most important phase of water damage restoration, and the one untrained responders most often get wrong. Once standing water has been extracted, a great deal of moisture is still held inside the structure: in insulation, concrete, subfloors, wood framing, drywall and other components. A structure left wet ends up with odors, wood rot, mold and failing materials, so the moisture has to be removed in a scientific way using dehumidification and controlled evaporation.

The structural drying technicians at Phoenix Restoration Co are IICRC-certified and trained in psychrometry, the science of how temperature, humidity and moisture in air relate. For each job they apply it to calculate airflow patterns, temperature settings and the placement of equipment that will give the best conditions for evaporation. Different materials (drywall, hardwood, concrete, plaster) dry at different rates and need different approaches to reach acceptable moisture levels without causing additional damage.

Daily monitoring is built into how we dry a structure, and it relies on calibrated meters, thermal cameras and thermo-hygrometers. The result is a detailed log of readings that tracks how moisture comes down over time in each material that got wet. Each day we use those numbers to reposition or retune the equipment so drying stays efficient. A surface that feels dry to your hand proves nothing, so the job is finished only once all affected materials test within the acceptable standards set by IICRC S500.

The Benefits of Professional Structural Drying

There is real science behind structural drying, which is why guessing does not work. Secondary mold damage most often traces back to someone who put down fans and hoped things would dry. To do it professionally, you have to know how each material takes on and gives up moisture, work out the grain depression, meaning the moisture removal capacity, for the job, and check progress every day. Get it wrong in one direction and mold feeds on leftover moisture, get it wrong in the other and wood or similar materials are damaged by over-drying.

Consumer products and the equipment professionals dry structures with have very little in common. A commercial LGR dehumidifier, short for low-grain refrigerant, pulls 10 to 20 times as much moisture out of the air as a residential one, while high-velocity air movers set up the exact airflow that speeds evaporation off structural surfaces. Machines like these cost a great deal and take training to use well, which explains why professional results are so much better than a do-it-yourself effort.

How to Prevent Structural Drying Problems

Taking preventive measures can help you avoid costly water damage issues down the road. Follow these tips from our IICRC-certified technicians:

  • Moisture can sit deep in a material whose surface feels dry, so rely on the meter readings
  • Let air movers and dehumidifiers run continuously, 24/7, until the technician removes them
  • If a new damp spot or smell shows up while drying is underway, let the technician know at once
  • Do not place personal items on top of drying equipment or block the airflow from air movers
  • Make sure the technician can get in each day for monitoring visits
  • Leave the drying equipment on even if the noise bothers you, since each hour counts

When to Call for Structural Drying in Phoenix

Recognizing the warning signs early can save you time, money, and prevent significant damage to your property. Watch for these telltale signs that professional help is needed:

  • A stale, musty smell that lingers even though the water was extracted
  • Hardwood floors that are cupping, crowning or showing gaps between boards
  • Drywall that is soft, swollen or shows moisture staining
  • Swollen trim and baseboards separating at the seams
  • Moisture meter readings above normal in walls, floors or ceilings after water damage
  • Beads of moisture on windows and cold surfaces after the water event
  • Humidity that will not come down in the affected space, whatever ventilation you try
