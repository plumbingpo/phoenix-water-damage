Appliance Leak Water Damage Help in Phoenix

Nearly every appliance leak we handle comes from one of three sources. Washer supply hoses can burst and keep spraying at full pressure until the valve is closed. A dishwasher tends to leak slowly from a door seal or drain hose, out of sight under the cabinets. Behind a refrigerator, the thin tube feeding the ice maker may drip for months. Cleanup is different for each of them.

On every Phoenix job, the first step is moving the appliance and tracing the path the water took. In kitchens that usually means under the cabinets and beneath the flooring. In laundry rooms it means the wall behind the machine and the rooms next door, or the ceiling below if the laundry is upstairs. Then our team removes the standing water, meters the materials that soaked it up, and places equipment matched to the size of the damage.

Do This First

Before We Arrive

  1. Turn Off the ApplianceStop the cycle and unplug the machine if the outlet and floor around it are dry. If not, switch it off at the breaker instead.
  2. Shut Off the Water LineWashers have hot and cold valves on the wall behind them. Dishwashers shut off under the sink. The small valve for an ice maker line sits behind the refrigerator or underneath.
  3. Soak Up Standing WaterGetting reachable water up with a mop or towels stops it from moving under the cabinets and the floor. Use a vacuum only if it is a wet and dry model.
  4. Keep the Door ClosedDo not open a washer or dishwasher that is still full. Opening the door mid-cycle can release the whole load of water onto the floor.
  5. Document What FailedKeep the part that failed, whether a hose, a fitting or a valve, and get photos of it. Your insurer and the appliance maker may both ask to see it.
Will Insurance Pay?

Does Insurance Cover It?

A hose that bursts or a machine that overflows without warning is the kind of sudden and accidental event homeowners policies often cover. The policy typically pays for the water damage, not for the appliance or the failed part. Slow seepage from a dishwasher or ice maker line is a harder claim, because gradual leaks usually are not covered. Certain insurers will want to know the hose's age and whether it was kept up. Check your policy, keep the failed part and take photos before cleanup.

Why It Happens

How It Usually Starts

  • Plastic or copper ice maker lines that kink, crack or loosen at the fitting.
  • Installation mistakes like a loose supply line connection or a dishwasher that does not sit level.
  • Worn dishwasher door gaskets, cracked drain hoses or a failed pump seal.
  • A clogged defrost drain or cracked drain pan that lets a refrigerator leak onto the floor.
  • Too much detergent or an overloaded drum that makes the machine overflow.
What Is at Risk

What Happens if You Wait

Mold Behind Appliances

Hardly anyone looks at the dark, warm space behind a dishwasher or refrigerator. Long before it is discovered, a slow drip back there can be growing mold across the floor and wall.

Rooms Next Door

Water may pour from a washer hose for hours on end. That water moves beneath walls into bedrooms, closets and hallways, soaking pad and carpet far from the laundry room.

Floors and What Is Under Them

Hardwood, vinyl plank and laminate all let water slip underneath. Cupping and swelling show up in the boards, and the subfloor can stay damp long after the surface appears dry.

Cabinet Damage

Toe kicks and cabinet sides take on water from underneath when a dishwasher leaks. Panels swell and come apart, and a wet cabinet box can keep the wall behind it damp for weeks.

Our Process

What Happens After You Call

1

Move the Appliance and Inspect

The appliance is disconnected and moved, and the supply is confirmed off. Then thermal imaging and moisture meters show us where water went below flooring, under the cabinets and into the walls nearby.

2

Extract the Water

Carpet and floors are cleared of standing water with extraction equipment. To reach water caught below cabinets or under floating floors, our crew pulls toe kicks or takes up boards.

3

Dry in Place

Dehumidifiers and air movers are set up, and when it is possible, directed airflow dries hardwood and cabinets where they sit so nothing has to be torn out.

4

Clean and Confirm

We apply antimicrobial treatment wherever discharge from the washer or the dishwasher made contact. Final moisture readings are taken before we return the appliance, so it never sits on a damp floor.
