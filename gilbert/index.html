How Long Drying Takes in a Gilbert Home

A wet building left alone does dry eventually, but far too slowly. Moisture inside walls, studs and subfloors can linger for weeks, and that is plenty of time for mold to grow and wood to distort. Our job on every Gilbert property is to speed that up in a controlled way.

Extraction is always step one, since pumping and vacuuming remove water much faster than any drying machine. Then air movers are set along the walls to push a fast stream of air across wet surfaces and lift moisture out of them. Dehumidifiers pull that moisture out of the air and send it down a drain, which keeps the room dry enough for evaporation to continue. Without the dehumidifiers, the air movers would just move damp air around.

A technician measures the wet materials daily against readings from an unaffected area of the property. Most homes in Gilbert reach that dry standard in 3 to 5 days, with dense materials like hardwood or plaster taking longer. We remove the machines when the readings reach the goal, and not before.

What Usually Goes Wrong in Gilbert Homes

Some water problems need a phone call this minute, and others can safely wait until morning. We have ordered these five common Gilbert calls by how fast each one needs attention.

Bursts: A pipe that has split is the most urgent call there is, because it puts out water for as long as the supply is on. Cold weather, depending on the season, is one trigger, with the leak appearing as the frozen section thaws. Shut the house supply, run a tap to drain the lines and phone us immediately, whatever the hour. Ceilings that bulge with trapped water should be left alone and the room below kept empty.

Runoff: Storm water coming under a door or through a garage is next in line. There is no valve to close here, so lift what you can off the floor and avoid wet electrical outlets. This water has crossed lawns, driveways and streets, which makes it unsanitary. The Phoenix Restoration Co team extracts it once conditions are safe, then removes soaked materials that cannot be cleaned.

Kitchens: Leaks from a dishwasher, an ice maker line or the plumbing under the kitchen sink should be dealt with the day you find them. Close the small valve under the sink or behind the appliance and the flow usually stops. The harm continues, though, since water has already run under cabinets and flooring where air does not move. For Gilbert kitchen jobs, our crew can frequently save the cabinets by pushing dry air in at the toe kick.

Crawlspaces: Moisture under a raised floor is seldom an emergency, but it should not be put off for a season. When we look underneath, we tend to find water lying on the liner, insulation that is soaked or sweating ducts. Given time, that humidity works its way upstairs and the joists start to rot. Our crew clears any standing water, brings the framing down to a normal moisture level and reports the likely cause.

Odors: Last on the list is a stale, earthy odor in a room that once had a leak, which is worth a scheduled visit. Mold can begin within 24 to 48 hours of a material getting wet, so a leak from months ago had plenty of time. Do not scrub or spray a large patch yourself, because disturbing it spreads spores through the house. Our technicians test the area, contain it and remove the growth along with the moisture feeding it across Gilbert.
