Crawl Space Water Removal: What Phoenix Homeowners Should Know

A flooded crawl space rarely announces itself. The usual clues are chilly, clammy floors, an earthy odor rising from the registers, or a pool of water waiting behind the access door. At that point the batts are typically saturated and the framing overhead has sat damp for several days. Crews from our company extract the water, haul away anything that cannot be saved and dry the structure ahead of rot.

Pumping is just half of the work. Because lumber under a floor is slow to soak and slow to dry, our crew tracks its moisture content with a meter and runs dehumidification for the 3 to 5 days it normally takes to bring the readings down. Finding the cause matters just as much, since a dripping water line, a failed drain or groundwater from a storm will flood the area again unless it is addressed.

Warning Signs

  • Corroded metal such as duct straps, pipe hangers and steel posts
  • Water beading and dripping off cold pipes or metal ducts under the floor
  • Batts of insulation drooping or dangling from the underside of the floor
  • A musty, earthy smell that is strongest near the floor vents
  • More crickets, roaches or other bugs showing up near the floor level
  • Joists, beams or subfloor panels coated with whitish or blackish fuzz

What You Cannot See

What Hides Behind It

Rot You Cannot See

The first soft wood is usually hidden, up where joists meet the subfloor or back at the sill plate. Our crew tests the lumber by hand to locate weak areas.

Electrical and Duct Hazards

Electrical runs, junction boxes and ductwork pass through most crawl spaces. When duct insulation is saturated it sends stale air around the home, while flooded wiring creates a real risk of shock.

Contaminated Water

Water under a house is often not clean. A leaking drain line or groundwater carrying soil and waste is Category 2 or 3 and needs protective gear and disinfection.

Termites and Other Pests

Damp wood and wet soil are exactly what termites, carpenter ants and rodents look for. Getting moisture levels down takes away a main reason for them to stay.

How We Fix It

What Happens After You Call

1

Inspect and Make Safe

We check for electrical hazards, sagging ducts and contaminated water before anyone crawls in. Then we map how far the water reached and take moisture readings on the joists and subfloor.

2

Remove Standing Water

We run pumps and extraction lines until the pooled water is gone, low pockets and trenches along the footings included. Any silt or mud sitting on the liner gets removed during this step.

3

Remove Wet Materials

We bag up the wet insulation, damaged vapor barrier and loose debris and take it all outside. Where mold has begun or is likely, the bare joists and subfloor get cleaned and an antimicrobial applied.

4

Dry and Verify

Low profile air movers and a dehumidifier run under the house until the wood reaches a normal moisture reading. A final meter check follows, and you receive the numbers along with steps to prevent a repeat.

Why Speed Matters

How the Damage Progresses

First day

Puddles Form Underneath

Low areas fill first while the ground underneath takes on water. Humidity in the space jumps within hours as the insulation pulls water into itself.

24 to 48 hours

Mold Gets Started

Mold can start on damp joists and paper faced insulation. Wet batts get heavy and begin to pull away from the subfloor.

Day 3 to 7

Moisture Moves Upstairs

Humid air from below works its way up into the rooms. You might see floorboards cupping and notice a musty odor while the framing continues to soak.

After a week

Wood Starts Rotting

Decay begins in any framing that never got a chance to dry. Joists and sill plates soften, fasteners rust and the damp space attracts termites and other pests.
